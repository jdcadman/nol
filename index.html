Day One  ·  Wednesday

Arrival in New Orleans

Evening

Land at MSY & Transfer to the Hotel

Your adventure begins the moment you touch down at Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport. A transfer brings you straight into the heart of the city and through the doors of one of America's grandest hotels.

Check-In

The Roosevelt New Orleans

A Waldorf Astoria Hotel and a city icon since 1893. Forty-foot ceilings, gilded columns, and the legendary Sazerac Bar — this is not just a place to sleep. This is the beginning of the experience. You'll be here for three nights.

3-night stay. One of America's most storied grand hotels.

Hotel Website

Day Two  ·  Thursday

The Historic French Quarter

Morning

Café Du Monde

Start the day the only correct way: café au lait and a cloud of powdered beignets at this legendary 1862 French Market institution. Go early, wear dark clothing, and don't rush.

Café Du Monde
Midday

French Quarter Walking Exploration

Wander the iron lace balconies and shuttered courtyards of the Quarter. Drift down Royal Street's antique shops, pause at Jackson Square, and let the city tell its stories in the language of architecture.

French Quarter Guide
Lunch

Central Grocery & Deli

The birthplace of the muffuletta — that glorious round sandwich stuffed with Italian meats, provolone, and olive salad. A New Orleans pilgrimage that's been worth it since 1906. (923 Decatur St)

View on Map
Dinner

Arnaud's Jazz Bistro

Sophisticated Creole cuisine in a room that has changed little since 1918. Expect shrimp remoulade, oysters Bienville, and live jazz that drifts between the white tablecloths and vintage photographs. Dress the part.

Attire: Business Casual

Arnaud's Restaurant

Day Three  ·  Friday

Garden District & Uptown Charm

Morning

St. Charles Streetcar Ride

Board America's oldest continuously operating street railway and glide beneath a canopy of live oaks from the CBD into the Garden District and Uptown. The world beyond the window tells the story of the city's layers.

St. Charles Line Info
Morning

Audubon Park

A 350-acre urban sanctuary where ancient live oaks drip with Spanish moss and the air smells like earth and magnolia. Stroll, breathe, and let the South's slow tempo do its work on you.

Audubon Park
Midday

Garden District Book Shop

Tucked inside The Rink, this beloved independent bookshop specializes in Southern literature, regional history, and signed editions. The perfect place to find a book that smells like the city it describes.

Garden District Book Shop
Afternoon

Witches Brew Cemetery Tour

An insider's walking tour through the city's iconic above-ground "Cities of the Dead" and the grandest mansions of the Garden District. History, ghost stories, and the eerie beauty of New Orleans' relationship with death.

Witches Brew Tours
Evening

The Jazz Playhouse

Located inside the Royal Sonesta, this intimate "listening room" hosts the city's finest jazz performers in an atmosphere that feels less like a bar and more like a conversation between genius and the dark.

The Jazz Playhouse

Day Four  ·  Saturday

From the River to The Countryside

Morning

Paddlewheeler Creole Queen

A 2.5-hour cruise on the mighty Mississippi aboard a working paddlewheel riverboat. The city's skyline, the river's breadth, the levees and history — all of it unfolds from the water in a way no street can offer.

Creole Queen Cruises
Afternoon

Scenic Drive — Across Lake Pontchartrain

Depart the city and cross the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway — the world's longest bridge over open water, nearly 24 miles of highway suspended over nothing but sky and lake — toward St. Francisville and the quieter South.

View on Map
Check-In

The Myrtles Plantation

One of America's most haunted homes — and one of its most beautiful. Built in 1796, this National Historic Landmark draped in Spanish moss is your home for two nights. Some guests check out with more than memories.

2-night stay. Historic, legendary, and possibly inhabited by more than the living.

The Myrtles Plantation
Dinner

Restaurant 1796

On-site at The Myrtles, this hearth-driven kitchen serves upscale Southern cuisine in a setting that feels part dining room, part living history. Wood smoke, cast iron, and the kind of hospitality the South invented.

Restaurant 1796

Day Five  ·  Sunday

St. Francisville & Southern History

Morning

Exploring St. Francisville

This small river town, built on bluffs above the Mississippi, moves at its own pace. Hilly streets, antebellum storefronts, and centuries of stories fill just a few charming blocks. Don't rush. The town won't let you.

St. Francisville Tourism
Afternoon

Rosedown Plantation State Historic Site

371 acres of formal gardens inspired by the great estates of France and Italy. Ancient camellias, magnolias, and live oaks frame the 1835 manor house. A place of extraordinary beauty and complicated, essential history.

Rosedown State Historic Site
Evening

The Myrtles Mystery Tour

As dusk settles over the grounds, your guide leads you through the legends, the documented hauntings, and the true history of The Myrtles. This is the tour people talk about long after they've returned home.

Mystery Tour Info

Day Six  ·  Monday

The River Road & Farewell

10:00 AM

Whitney Plantation

The only plantation museum in Louisiana dedicated entirely to the lives and stories of the enslaved people who built this world. A guided experience through memorials, original cabins, and the weight of true history. Essential and unforgettable.

Whitney Plantation
Lunch

Wayne Jacob's Smokehouse

In LaPlace — the andouille capital of the world — this family smokehouse has been doing one thing beautifully for generations. Authentic Cajun smoked meats, boudin, and the kind of lunch that requires no menu explanation.

View on Map
Afternoon

Joyce Wildlife Swampwalk

A self-guided elevated boardwalk that carries you deep into the primordial silence of the Manchac Swamp. Cypress knees, alligators, and the particular hush of a world that has existed for millennia. A perfect last memory of Louisiana.

Joyce Wildlife Area
Departure

Transfer to MSY → Home

The road back to Louis Armstrong Airport carries all of it with you — the beignet dust, the jazz, the Spanish moss, the ghost stories, the smoke. Safe travels, and until next time.
